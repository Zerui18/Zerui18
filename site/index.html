Search Pathology Diagnosis for Automated Discovery

Alistair Cheong* · Zerui Chen* (*equal contribution)
Automated discovery systems are usually compared on the score of their best artifact, which says little about the search that produced it. We introduce the provenance hypergraph, a system-agnostic abstraction of a search trace, and detectors for two pathologies on it: redundant rediscovery (an exploration failure) and poorly grounded next steps (an exploitation failure), selected on five released meta-evaluation datasets. Applied to our open re-implementation of TTT-Discover on the Erdős minimum-overlap problem, the readings guided a memory intervention that reached the baseline's final score with 28% fewer solutions, and diagnosed why a second intervention failed. To be released as ADSInspector, an ADS-agnostic Python package, after notification.

Evolution Fine-Tuning: Learning to Discover Across 371 Optimization Tasks

Young-Jun Lee · Seungone Kim · Minki Kang · Alistair Cheong · Zerui Chen · Seungho Han · Taehee Jung · Dongyeop Kang
EFT is a mid-training paradigm that teaches LLMs to evolve solutions across tasks by converting evolutionary-search trajectories into supervision: a 156K-trajectory dataset over 371 tasks in 10 domains, with fine-tuned 2B–9B models gaining +10.2% on average across 22 held-out tasks and, paired with test-time RL, matching state-of-the-art on two circle-packing tasks. My part: nanodiscover was the test-time-RL scaffold behind the test-time-RL results.

02 — Projects

A handful of things I've built and still care about.

nanodiscover↗ Ray+vLLM · DeepSpeed · Slurm · AWS

An open re-implementation of TTT-Discover, a state-of-the-art test-time-learning discovery system (evolutionary search with an LLM proposer that the reference system trains online). Replaces the Tinker API dependency (~$500 per problem) with local vLLM/DeepSpeed/Ray inference and training, fixes upstream bugs, and reproduces the published results up to sampling stochasticity. Adopted as the test-time-RL scaffold in Evolution Fine-Tuning (co-authored; Oral, AID-Wild @ ACM CAIS 2026) and the case-study system in our search-pathology paper.

zlab↗ PyTorch · Lightning · Pandas

A modular deep learning research platform for rapid experimentation with transformer architectures, enabling systematic ablation studies across attention mechanisms, positional encodings, and training configurations via a registry-driven composition system. Designed for seq2seq tasks (machine translation, speech-to-text) with built-in experiment management for grid search and metric-tracked evaluation.

Frame & Frame App↗ UIKit · SwiftUI · CoreData · Cydia Substrate

An iOS jailbroken tweak, app, and cli to play videos as wallpaper on lock & home screens. Performance optimized & video catalogue built-in. Checkout the release posts on Reddit to see it in action: V2 · V3

MML Book↗ NumPy · SymPy · Matplotlib · LaTeX

Typesetting the full solutions to Mathematics for Machine Learning, implementing with basic NumPy ops the core linear algebra, regression and bayesian inference algorithms.

03 — Prog Langs

What I reach for these days.

Python
My everyday workhorse for research runs and scripting. I enjoy the freedom of its extreme dynamism until my function preconditions take more than half of the function, and that borrowed freedom is revealed to be a debt I repay by hand.
Swift
My first language, on my first MacBook! A decade of building iOS apps. Its syntax, type system and semantics used to be elegant and powerful like the phone it runs on. Then it started trying to be every language at once.
Objective-C
Feels like a preprocessor + runtime bolted onto C. Works as a bridge from Swift into the C-world now, but it handed jailbreak tweak developers the lifeline: dynamic message dispatch, runtime classes and methods one can edit at will, and binaries that can be dumped into readable headers.
TypeScript
My way onto the web, being able to do arithmetic on types was my new favourite superpower for a while (wdym the type system is turing-complete?! someone wrote a type checker in it).
C++
The language and its std lib have the most obscure semantics of any language I know. Wdym std::move doesn't move anything? enable_if promoted a SFINAE hack into the std lib, until Concepts finally gave template constraints a real place in the language. I'll never know all of it, and that's chill.
Rust
The latest language I've learnt, from the Book. For the first time ownership and moves made complete sense, and aliasing-xor-mutability made every violation visible at compile time. It also has just the right slice of modern ergonomics in its syntax and std lib to be a pleasure to write.
Java
A timeless classic that I learnt in Tripos and have yet to justify using it over the others for any project. The JVM is the best runtime I never get to use.
OCaml
Learnt it during Tripos too. Broke my mind at first, but I grew to love the elegance of functional programming: it's almost mathematical yet still practical. Its Hindley–Milner type inference feels like an oracle, and code generality comes free rather than painstakingly declared.
LaTeX
What I type the most nowadays, after Python. For writing papers and notes, or interpreting the unrendered math blocks in agents' responses.
